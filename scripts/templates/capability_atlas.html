  4. The largest single risk is an unknown bypass route. Runtime surface completeness (CAP-022) is NOT_ESTABLISHED; a process with its own credentials makes every other guarantee local. assessment
  5. Activation must be described per capability and profile. In the supported, enforcing execution configuration the token, payload binding and dispatch are on the path; durable audit, strict signature checking and actual isolation still depend on configuration. Other capabilities are opt-in behind flags or policy. Executed actions, spent tokens and chain records cannot be rolled back. Budget history is not deleted; a reservation can only be released through a documented resolution once it is established that the effect did not happen.
  6. The next work is CAP-031 and one safe configuration, before all 32 are built out in the API and SDK. See the repair order under pilot readiness. assessment
  7. The research components must not gain authority. AROMER (CAP-012) and the WS1 monitor (CAP-030) become more useful when their evidence and failure modes are visible in the API, and more dangerous if they are treated as authority.

Immediate NO-GO

  • a known unmediated write route
  • an unsigned ToolSpec or a mutable permissive policy
  • a stale or missing shared budget ledger with several hosts
  • privilege escalation through a research component
  • a silent retry of an unknown payment or effect

Recommended way forward

  • Freeze one named configuration and one harmless tool set.

  • The first canary is a read or non-actuating tool.

Scope for reversal

Can be reversed

  • Activation: opt-in capabilities can be turned off by flag or policy. Token, payload binding and dispatch are part of the supported enforcing execution configuration and are not turned off there; configuration-dependent parts (durable audit, strict signature, isolation) can change, but only after privileged actions are stopped first. With the manifest, mode becomes a signed, versioned value.
  • Modes: ENFORCE can be lowered to SHADOW without data loss, but the protection goes with it. Stop privileged actions before such a downgrade.
  • Topology: Postgres can be switched back to SQLite only for technical review on one host, never in a multi-host pilot.
  • SDK: additive methods; existing methods are kept through migration.

Cannot be reversed

  • Executed effects in tools, rails or plants.
  • Spent tokens and lease nonces: they stay spent.
  • The audit chain's records: append-only.
  • The budget history: not deleted. UNKNOWN reservations are held until a documented resolution has established whether the effect happened; only then can a reservation be released.

Rollback after the SDD: stop new writes, keep the audit and the UNKNOWN reservations, drain reconciliations, and keep the exact old signed policy, ToolSpec and schema for reconstruction. A rollback never deletes spent grants and never treats an unobserved action as not executed.

Risk assessment

Each capability is placed by the likelihood that something goes wrong and the consequence if it does, given the SDD's intended mode assessment. High consequence almost always follows from the capability sitting on the authority or effect path.

Cross-cutting risks

  • Bypass outside the PEP: a process with direct credentials. Mitigation: CAP-022 in an external host, CAP-024 three-domain split, egress control.
  • Local state across hosts: a SQLite ledger split between hosts gives an overrun. Mitigation: Postgres and NO-GO without a shared ledger.
  • UNKNOWN treated as not executed: double effect. Mitigation: never retry; resolution only from an authoritative source.
  • A model given authority: a monitor or AROMER lowers a refusal. Mitigation: INV-02, SHADOW mode.
  • False independence: the same administrator owns recorder and executor. Mitigation: a separate trust domain, honest labelling.
  • Symmetric keys where they are still used: the A2A envelope (CAP-006) and audit signing are HMAC, and grants accept HMAC only when REMORA_PDP_ACCEPT_HMAC is explicitly enabled or when no Ed25519 key is configured. Grants (v3) and leases have Ed25519. Mitigation: verify-only in the execution domain, HMAC refused in the pilot, documented key distribution.

Risks of the adoption itself assessment

  • A new manifest becomes a fourth catalogue if it repeats the register and the declaration. SDD v1.1 limits it to deployment fields.
  • Changes to servers/execution_api.py and lease.py touch many capabilities at once.
  • The G-gates can be read as an alternative to the REM requirements. SDD v1.1 says they can only be added through their own changes.
  • Calendar risk: REM-021 needs an independent person and cannot be solved with code.

How security is kept

The SDD locks twelve invariants. They build on mechanisms that already exist and make them mandatory in the pilot profile.

InvariantRequirementCarrying capabilities
INV-01Single PEP: privileged effects go only through the approved executorCAP-013, CAP-024
INV-02No model authority: monitors, oracles, AROMER cannot lower a refusal or mint a grantCAP-001, CAP-012, CAP-030
INV-03Exact action: tenant, principal, task, target, ToolSpec, arguments, policy, capability set and limits are boundCAP-004, CAP-018, CAP-023, CAP-031
INV-04Freshness (proposed requirement): REMORA's own authority state is re-checked at dispatch. Local admission can be atomic; the external effect is not atomic with the authority databaseCAP-003, CAP-007, CAP-020
INV-05Unknown is not negative: no blind replay of UNKNOWNCAP-013, CAP-031
INV-06Durable across workersCAP-003, CAP-005, CAP-031
INV-07Evidence is not authorityCAP-019, CAP-025, CAP-026..029
INV-08Downgrade-resistant activation: a missing premise refuses startupCAP-017, manifest
INV-09Tenant separation through read, list, export and cacheCAP-009
INV-10Testable claims; stale evidence cannot promote a releaseCAP-META, CAP-015
INV-11Every effect assertion names observer, vantage and methodCAP-021, CAP-022
INV-12Private signing keys stay out of client and executor where custody requires itCAP-017, CAP-024

Layer on layer

  • Authentication and RBAC (CAP-009)
  • Deterministic decision with invariants (CAP-001, CAP-015)
  • Minimized capability set and task binding (CAP-023, CAP-018)
  • Human approval with a fresh re-gate (CAP-007)
  • Single-use token and exact call (CAP-003, CAP-004)
  • Premises, limits and lease at dispatch (CAP-020, CAP-031, CAP-013)
  • Mediated effect in its own domain (CAP-024)
  • Evidence in the chain and outside the process (CAP-005, CAP-021)

What security does not cover

  • Correctness of the model's intent.
  • Visibility into every host path without an external observer.
  • Independent proof of physical effect based on the producer's own logs.
  • Real financial clearing or regulatory certification.
